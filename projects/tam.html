The situation

A high-growth business with fragmented institutional data and uneven penetration across segments. Territory assignments were opinion-driven and forecasting wasn't grounded in any segmentation. Leadership needed a defensible answer to one question: "Where should we focus, and why?" — while rep coverage stayed misaligned and upside and risk stayed hidden.

Scope

Design and implement a TAM-driven prioritization system to:

  • Calculate institution-level TAM adjusted for fit and conversion likelihood.
  • Segment accounts by penetration and revenue potential.
  • Identify expansion, defend, optimize, and de-prioritize tiers.
  • Drive territory realignment, rep focus, forecast assumptions, and resource allocation.

The 12-box

The core artifact: a matrix of revenue potential × penetration. Each cell carries a prescribed action, turning a spreadsheet of accounts into a prioritized playbook.

How it was built

  1. Construct account-level TAM, adjusted for fit and conversion likelihood.
  2. Calculate penetration as actual revenue ÷ TAM.
  3. Build the 12-box matrix: revenue/potential tier × penetration level.
  4. Derive prioritization tiers and actions — expand, defend, optimize, de-prioritize.
  5. Roll up to territory level for imbalance analysis and reallocation modeling.
  6. Embed the output into the forecast and executive visibility processes.

Data sources

  • Enrollment / firmographic size from institutional data feeds.
  • Historical revenue and product penetration from CRM / ERP.
  • Adoption patterns and discipline distribution from usage logs.
  • Territory alignment and growth trends from the sales-ops database.

All normalized to account-level granularity via SQL joins and Python preprocessing, then validated against the standard data-integrity gates →

What it found

  • A concentration of high-TAM accounts sitting in low-penetration tiers — major expansion left on the table.
  • Territories overloaded with low-potential accounts, misaligning rep capacity.
  • Reps defending saturated accounts with minimal upside — reallocation created headroom.
  • Mid-tier accounts with high growth potential but low engagement — targeted plays unlocked incremental revenue.

The real deliverable wasn't a chart — it was a shared, defensible language for where revenue attention should go.
